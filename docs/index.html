Energy levels of the NV

The NV centre (NV⁻ to be precise) has an extra electron which forms a spin S=1 pair with one of the vacancy electrons, inducing 3 spin states: ∣0> "anti parallel", ∣+1> "up", and ∣-1> "down".
These exist in 2 energy levels, the ground state and excited state, so we have the triplet ground state ³A and triplet excited state ³E.
There are also 2 intermediate singlet states, ¹A and ¹E, which can be used during the transition from ³E → ³A. See below the energy diagram taken from Wikipedia.

The most stable state is ³A with spin state ∣0>, as it has the lowest energy. We can optically pump the NV centre by applying a green laser (~532nm) to transition to the excited state. This preserves the spin state. From ³E ∣0> the system relaxes to ³A ∣0> by emitting a red photon (~637nm). We can also manipulate the spin state by applying a microwave frequency of ~2.87GHz (called the zero-field splitting) to arrive at ³A ∣±1>. We also call such a frequency an electron spin resonance (ESR) frequency. If this state is now excited via the laser, it also transitions back from ³E ∣±1> to ³A ∣±1> by emitting a red photon, but only ~70% of the time. The other 30% it relaxes via the intermediate singlet states without emitting a photon.
Hence, we observe a dip in fluorescence when optically pumping the system while in ∣±1> compared to ∣0>, i.e., relating fluorescence to microwave frequency yields a dip at ~2.87GHz.

The spin states ∣+1> and ∣-1> have the same energy levels, given there is no magnetic field. However, in the presence of a magnetic field, the spin states split due to the Zeeman splitting. So now it takes a microwave frequency <2.87GHz to go from ∣0> to ∣-1> and >2.87GHz to go from ∣0>. Hence, we obvserve two dips when relating fluorescence to microwave frequency. We denote these two ESR frequencies by ESR- and ESR+, respectively.

Temperature dependence

Not only magnetic fields, but also other external factors influence the energy levels of the NV centre. Temperature, e.g., affects mainly the zero-field splitting: a higher temperature reduces the required energy to go from spin ∣0> to ∣±1>.

Ensembles of NV centres

A NV centre in diamond has an orientation given by the diamond lattice. It can be oriented along (1,1,1), (1,-1,-1), (-1,1,-1), or (-1,-1,1). In this visualisation we denote those orientations as 111, 100, 010, and 001. If we have a diamond with not just one but many NV centres, we speak of an ensemble. We can produce diamonds with specifically oriented NV centres, but in this visualisation we assume an equal distribution among all orientations.
The external magnetic field has then different influences on the different orientations: the more aligned the magnetic field vector with the orientation of the NV centre, the stronger the effect on the ESR frequencies.

Contrast

Along the vertical axis, we plot the normalised fluorescence. We assume an optimal contrast of ~30% for a single NV centre and around ~20% for an ensemble of NVs. For an ensemble the contrast is lower due to losses in the diamond and the 20% contrast is split onto the different orientations, i.e., every axis accounts for ~5% of the contrast. The maximum contrast can then only be reached when there is no external magnetic field, so all axis have an ESR at the zero-field splitting.
